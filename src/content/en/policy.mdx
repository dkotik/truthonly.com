# Communication Policy

I am a missionary staff member with ABWE. Everything here, however, is my personal opinion and is not read or approved by ABWE leadership or administration before it is posted. Opinions, conclusions and other information expressed here do not necessarily reflect the views of ABWE.

“Work-related social media” is defined as any activity conducted online through any form of social media in your capacity as an ABWE missionary staff, including activities conducted with an ABWE e-mail address, activity that can be traced back to ABWE’s domain name or servers, activity that uses ABWE assets/money (like paying for an advertisement, or registering an account with an ABWE email address), or activities that would otherwise be reasonably associated with ABWE’s name. All other social media activity is classified as personal social media activity and is not covered by this policy.

## Social Media

GMI recognizes that participation in social media may prove beneficial to some missionaries.
Social media can be a helpful way to communicate with your support team and keep people
informed as to your prayer requests, praise reports, news and needs. Used in the right way, social
media can be a useful tool for missionaries.
At the same time, improper or unwise use of social media can cause significant damage. There
can be damage to your ministry, the ministry of another missionary, the ministry of Grace
Community Church, or most significantly, it can bring disrepute on the name of Christ. We should
remember that “He who guards his mouth and his tongue, guards his soul from troubles”
(Proverbs 21:23).
Therefore, the following considerations should be made when using any type of social media:
1. Consider whether you are avoiding unnecessary offense.
In our current climate where any issue that comes up in society is highly polarized, it’s almost
impossible not to offend someone with social media posts. The gospel, by nature, offends
those who reject it. If your presence on social media is focused on the gospel, you will be
criticized. However, commenting on issues that are not strictly biblical (i.e., political views,
personal opinions on matters of conscience, etc.) can also result in conflict and offense that is
avoidable. If a missionary offends another person, it should only be with the clear teaching of
God’s Word. Furthermore, any criticism directed against the people or government of the
missionary’s host country can damage the missionary’s reputation among those to whom he is
seeking to minister or result in undesired attention from the host country’s government. This
may result in restrictions put on the ministry in that country or even expulsion from the country.
The missionary must maintain a focus on the ministry given to him by the Lord and avoid
secondary matters (1 Timothy 6:20–21; 2 Timothy 2:16).
2. Consider the qualification of being above reproach.
A missionary must be characterized as one who is above reproach (1 Timothy 3:1–7; Titus 1:5–
9). Therefore, there must be nothing in a missionary’s life that disqualifies him from being an
example in both moral and spiritual character. Whether it is the posting of pictures, statements
proclaimed, or comments given, everything should be done as those who seek to represent
Christ in everything and avoid any distraction from the gospel message. Areas that must be
avoided include:
• Immodesty of anyone in photographs. There should never be anything posted that would
be an occasion for a fellow believer to stumble in regard to the lust of the eyes (Matthew
18:6; 1 John 2:16).
• Coarse language or (mild) profanity. Such language is not fitting for any believer, much
less a missionary (Ephesians 5:3–4). If it is a word or phrase that would not be printed in the
church bulletin or in a letter to your mother, it should not be posted.
• Frivolous or undignified. A missionary should be sober and dignified, sound in speech
and behavior that is above reproach (1 Timothy 3:4; Titus 1:6–8). If it is questionable, it is
probably best not posted to social media.
• Expressions of anger or discontent. Missionaries should fight the temptation to use social
media as catharsis, to let the world know of their anger or frustration. Often an emotional
response to a situation will result in sinful speech (Proverbs 10:19). Not everyone needs to
know your opinion on the matters of the day (Proverbs 18:2). The mature believer will face
disappointments or disagreements by taking it to the Lord in prayer (Philippians 4:6;
Colossians 4:2; 1 Peter 4:7).
3. Consider the calling as a missionary sent by GMI.
As a member of GMI, missionaries have been sent to not only represent Christ in all things, but
also as representatives GCC and GMI. The missionary should avoid any statements on social
media that may lead others to conclude that he is taking a doctrinal position contrary to GCC.
Any concerns or disagreements in doctrine or action taken by GMI or GCC can be discussed
with the Team Leader, Regional Shepherd, or an ECHO elder (Hebrews 13:17). Opinions that
are critical of GCC of GMI should be discussed privately, not aired publicly.
4. Consider whether it is the right forum for the statement.
Many types of social media allow for a limited amount of explanation or defense of a
statement. Another means may be better suited for the statement that you want to make.
Writing disparaging remarks about another person or organization in a public manner is rarely
the most appropriate forum. It is usually most gracious and profitable to discuss the matter
directly to the person. Furthermore, it should be remembered that once something is on the
Internet, it’s there forever and accessible by anyone. If you would not be comfortable having a
post shared in the church bulletin or on the front page of a newspaper, it should not be posted
online.
5. Consider the possible security implications.
There have been many missionaries who have had their ministry cut short by sharing excessive
information on the internet. Even statements made before a missionary arrives on the field
have resulted in a country refusing entry. Consider that your public presence on the internet
may preclude you from ever ministering in a closed access country. Additionally, avoid
identifying others on social media as members of GMI, as you may compromise their ministry
in other parts of the world.
If you are concerned that another missionary is posting things on social media that violate these
policies, first contact that missionary directly and express your concerns. If he or she continues to
post questionable content, or if the violation of this policy is particularly egregious, please contact
the GMI home office.
PS “Social media” is defined as any facility for online publication and commentary, including
blogs, wikis and social networking sites such as Facebook, LinkedIn, Twitter, Flickr and YouTube.
These principles also apply to newsletters and presentations.
